# Interview Transcript - 1

**Interviewer:**  
Think about your experience forming a team for a class project. What went well, and what did not?

**Participant:**  
I think, from several times forming a team, the first one was last semester. At the time, I was just thinking that forming a team was about being comfortable with the people.

Now, I also have group work, but I feel like when I try to make a group, it is not because I'm close to them, but because of my interests, how approachable they are, and how critical they are.

I think about how they will help me to have a good mark, because reflecting from last semester, I feel like I was carrying the whole thing. I was the only one who had a background in Geographic Information Science, so I felt like I had to teach people how to do it.

Even though they were very nice and I was comfortable with them, it made me realise that if I have group work, I have to be with ambitious and active people. So it's not just me teaching people; they can also teach me. It would benefit me.

I choose people who have a good GPA—not necessarily because of their GPA, but because they can help me pass the course with a good mark.

**Interviewer:**  
So basically, you reflected on your previous experience and changed your approach to forming a team this semester.

**Participant:**  
That's true.

**Interviewer:**  
What's the ideal team dynamic you look for in a class project?

**Participant:**  
I think the ideal team for a class project is a team where all of us are active. Not some of us being not really active and not caring about the whole process.

Sometimes people bail. They don't come to the group meetings, and they don't participate in the discussions.

When I form a team, I look at the whole course profile. If there's group work, I tell one of my friends who I know is really good at making assignments and getting a perfect score that fulfils the rubric. That's how I make my team.

**Interviewer:**  
Interesting. Have you ever not gotten a team, or been stuck with a random team?

**Participant:**  
Actually, for this semester, I got the same group as my friends. But since it was randomised from the lecture, it was still random. Fortunately, the people in the team are really approachable and active, so I feel like that's such a fortunate thing.

**Interviewer:**  
Very nice to hear that. If you were a tutor and had to divide the class into groups for a class project, how would you do it?

**Participant:**  
For me, I think I would just let people choose by themselves, because I know that if we are not really close with people, especially if there's a language barrier, some people are just shy to express themselves.

I don't want people to have a bad mark because of their team.

But if it happened and I had to divide people based on a random arrangement, I would definitely have a peer review, like a group review. So people can review each other, which I think works.

I think the last time, during my bachelor's, it also worked. We had one of the teammates who I felt was annoying and liked to do things her own way.

It wasn't personal because we had already discussed her behaviour, and when it came to the group review, she received a bad score, I would say.

**Interviewer:**  
Okay. So, did that make it better?

**Participant:**  
Not better, but I feel like it was fair enough to have a mark from the lecturer. The lecturer also made sure to ask us, "Is it true?" because maybe we were wrong.

But since it was based on the overall explanation, that's why she deserved that score.
