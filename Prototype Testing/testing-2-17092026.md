# Part 1 — Introduction & Prototype 1: Text/Riddle-Based Clues

**Facilitator:**

The activity is not exactly about forming a team. We want to help students engage with strangers.

When we first come into a class, everyone can be a stranger. Then the tutor says, "Make a team," and sometimes you're like, "Oh my God, how do I interact with people? How do I approach them first?"

So we're testing two different prototypes because we need to decide which one we'll bring forward for Week 12.

The idea is to help students interact with strangers and feel more comfortable.

**Participants:**

Okay.

**Facilitator:**

There are six cue cards. They contain puzzles or riddle-like statements.

At least two cards link to the same theme, making a team of two students. Since there are six of you, you'll end up with three teams of two.

I'll hand out the cards. You need to socialise with each other to find the people with the same theme and form the teams.

You can pick any card.

## Understanding the clues

**Facilitator:**

The card is a hint rather than a literal meaning. You can solve it by asking other people things like:

"What did you get?"
"What does that mean?"
"Is it the same place?"

Each pair of hints describes one place, and the places are UQ-specific.

# Part 4 — Discussion About the Concept

## Conversation Starter vs Team Formation

**Facilitator:**

What do you think about the concept?

**Participant 1:**

I feel like, as a conversation starter, it may be an interesting way to make strangers talk, especially if there's not much friction to start talking about those things.

But if I actually want to form a team and know whether I want to work with these people, there should be an extra step.

There should be more conversations.

**Participant 1:**

We kind of formed a team very quickly, but I never got to talk to those people.

The theme might not even be relevant to me.

I want to have an opportunity to talk with others.

At this stage, it still feels like the team formation is quite random.

It's not very different from being assigned to a table and saying, "Okay, let's form a team."

## Teams Are Determined by the Materials

**Participant 2:**

For me, both of them are fine as experiences. They start conversations.

But as a team formation exercise, they don't work.

Because the teams are in the materials, right?

The teams have nothing to do with the people.

I randomly picked a card. I didn't even get to choose how that worked.

There was nothing about me in either of the things that I got.

Which meant there was nothing for me to match with.

Nothing about me was matching with any of the other people I spoke to.

**Participant 2:**

Because the teams are embedded in the links that are brought to the exercise, you're randomly assigning teams and creating an icebreaker experience.

That's fine.

But in terms of a team formation experience, it's actually just a random assignment.

**Participant 3:**
